## [India's Supreme Court angry after junior judge cites fake AI-generated legal orders](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c178zzw780xo) ⭐️ 8.0/10

India's Supreme Court expressed anger after a junior judge submitted fake AI-generated legal orders in a case, highlighting serious accountability issues. The judge claimed the mistake occurred due to reliance on an automated source, but senior judges have threatened consequences over the inappropriate use of AI. This incident demonstrates the real-world legal consequences and professional accountability failures when AI is misused in critical fields like law. It underscores the urgent need for clear governance frameworks and human oversight as AI tools become more integrated into professional workflows, affecting judicial integrity and public trust. The judge defended herself by stating she had no intention to misrepresent rulings and that the error was due to reliance on an automatic source. This case follows a pattern of similar incidents globally where legal professionals have submitted AI-generated content containing fabricated citations or legal precedents.

In legal contexts, professionals have a duty to verify the accuracy of all submitted materials, and judicial officers have a specific duty to supervise staff and ensure appropriate use of technology. Several jurisdictions are now developing guardrails and policies to govern AI use in courts to prevent outcomes from being affected by unreliable AI-generated content.

## [China Telecom achieves 409km distributed AI training over multi-core fiber with 97% performance retention](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/LwS0WOK-uzL4cm0WtQ_IxA) ⭐️ 8.0/10

China Telecom Research Institute, Guangdong Telecom, and Huawei successfully conducted a cross-city interconnection test between major AI computing centers in Guangzhou and Shenzhen using multi-core fiber over a distance of 409.61 kilometers. The distributed large model training achieved up to 97% of the performance compared to centralized training, leveraging 800 Gb/s single-wavelength technology and the parallel transmission capabilities of multi-core fiber. This demonstration is significant because it proves the feasibility of efficiently connecting geographically dispersed AI computing centers over long distances, which is crucial for pooling computational resources and scaling AI training beyond the limits of a single data center. It represents a major step forward in optical networking infrastructure specifically tailored for the high-bandwidth, low-latency demands of distributed AI workloads. The test was conducted on China Telecom's latest pilot platform and operated within a complex, real-world network environment to ensure high-speed and stable transmission. The project also involved upgrading an integrated "optical-network-computing-application" architecture and implementing more granular traffic control to maintain stable training performance over long-distance networks.

telegram · zaihuapd · Mar 3, 05:30

**Background**: Multi-core fiber (MCF) is an optical fiber that contains multiple independent light-guiding cores within a single cladding, enabling parallel data transmission and significantly increasing the capacity of a single fiber strand compared to traditional single-core fibers. Single-wavelength 800 Gb/s technology represents a high-speed optical transmission standard, pushing the data rate carried by a single laser wavelength to 800 gigabits per second. The concept of an integrated "optical-network-computing" architecture aims to tightly couple optical transport networks with computing resources to optimize performance for applications like distributed AI training.

## [US Federal Judge Rules AI-Generated Artwork Ineligible for Copyright Protection](https://t.me/zaihuapd/39991) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A US federal judge has ruled that artwork created solely by artificial intelligence is not eligible for copyright protection, affirming the US Copyright Office's position. The ruling came in response to a lawsuit filed by an AI company CEO who argued that AI-generated works should be protected. This ruling establishes a significant legal precedent, clarifying that under current US copyright law, protection is reserved for human-authored creations. It will have immediate and broad implications for AI companies, artists, and content creators, shaping how AI-generated content is valued, used, and monetized across creative industries. The judge emphasized that human creativity is the cornerstone of copyright protection, and the degree of human involvement and creative control is a key factor in determining eligibility. This case is identified as Thaler v. Perlmutter, a notable legal test for the copyrightability of purely AI-generated outputs.

telegram · zaihuapd · Mar 3, 06:15

**Background**: US copyright law has a longstanding "human authorship requirement," meaning protection is granted to the "writings of authors," which historically refers to human creators. The US Copyright Office has maintained that works generated by AI without sufficient human creative input are not copyrightable. As generative AI tools become more advanced and widespread, the legal status of their outputs has become a critical and contested issue for policymakers, courts, and the creative community.

This partnership marks a significant step in the integration of cutting-edge commercial AI into national security and military operations, potentially setting a precedent for how such technology is governed. The explicit safety protocols aim to address ethical concerns and could influence future contracts between AI companies and government agencies globally. The deployment will use a cloud-only architecture, with OpenAI retaining control over the security stack and oversight by authorized personnel.
