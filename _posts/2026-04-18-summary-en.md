## [Chinese scientists create artificial ball lightning, confirming its nature as an electromagnetic soliton.](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260416/b487a8967b70495f8fc00364844225d6/c.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A research team from the Shanghai Institute of Optics and Fine Mechanics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, has for the first time artificially generated and captured a spherical luminous body highly similar to natural ball lightning. Their findings, published in Nature Photonics on April 16, confirm that ball lightning is an 'electromagnetic soliton'. This breakthrough provides the first experimental evidence for a long-standing scientific mystery, offering a new physical model to understand extreme electromagnetic energy confinement. It could have significant implications for research in fusion energy and high-energy-density physics by revealing fundamental mechanisms of energy confinement. The team used a strong laser to drive terahertz waves, creating relativistic field strengths that ionized argon gas into plasma. Through a balance of optical radiation pressure and thermal pressure, they confined the energy into a spherical ball approximately 100 micrometers in diameter with a lifespan of about 100 nanoseconds. Through physical scaling, this corresponds to a natural ball lightning event tens of centimeters in diameter lasting several seconds.

**Background**: Ball lightning is a rare and poorly understood atmospheric phenomenon described as a persistent, glowing sphere that appears during thunderstorms. An electromagnetic soliton is a special type of self-reinforcing, localized wave packet that maintains its shape and speed over long distances due to a precise balance between nonlinear and dispersive effects, behaving somewhat like a particle. Terahertz radiation refers to electromagnetic waves with frequencies between infrared and microwaves, and relativistic field strengths are fields so intense that they accelerate charged particles to speeds approaching the speed of light.

## [Nature study of 15,836 ancient genomes reveals widespread directional selection in humans over past 10,000 years](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10358-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A study published in Nature by researchers from Harvard Medical School and other institutions analyzed 15,836 ancient West Eurasian genomes and found that directional selection has been widespread in human evolution over the past 10,000 years. The research identified hundreds of alleles under strong selection, with frequency changes linked to decreased predicted body fat and schizophrenia risk, and increased cognitive performance indicators. This finding challenges the traditional view that advantageous mutation-driven directional selection has been rare in recent human evolution, fundamentally reshaping our understanding of how natural selection has continued to shape modern human traits. The study provides crucial evidence for how Darwinian natural selection has molded the genetic architecture of complex human traits in our recent evolutionary history. The study analyzed 15,836 ancient genomes including 10,016 newly sequenced samples, specifically focusing on West Eurasian populations. Researchers estimated selection coefficients for 9.7 million variant sites, providing quantitative measures of selection strength across the genome.

**Background**: Ancient DNA (aDNA) analysis involves extracting and sequencing genetic material from historical or archaeological remains, with recent technological advances enabling large-scale studies of ancient genomes. Directional selection is a form of natural selection where one phenotype is favored over others, causing allele frequencies to shift in a specific direction over generations. The selection coefficient (denoted as 's') is a population genetics measure that quantifies the relative fitness advantage or disadvantage of a particular genotype compared to others in the population.

## [Smol Machines launches portable VMs with subsecond cold starts as a potential Docker alternative.](https://github.com/smol-machines/smolvm) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new CLI tool called smolvm has been released, enabling users to create and run portable Linux virtual machines with sub-second cold start times and container-like ergonomics. The project, developed by a former AWS engineer, aims to combine the isolation of VMs with the speed and usability of containers. This innovation could significantly impact cloud computing and DevOps by offering stronger security isolation than containers while maintaining fast startup times, potentially positioning it as a high-performance alternative to Docker for certain workloads. It addresses the trade-off between the lightweight nature of containers and the robust isolation of traditional virtual machines. The tool can package applications into self-contained binaries (e.g., `smolvm pack create`), providing an isolated environment without needing language-specific version managers. It currently supports running custom Linux VMs locally on macOS and Linux systems, with features like elastic memory usage.

hackernews · binsquare · Apr 17, 17:18

**Background**: Containers and virtual machines are two common deployment technologies. Containers virtualize at the operating system level, sharing the host OS kernel, which makes them lightweight and fast to start. Virtual machines (VMs) virtualize the entire hardware, running a full guest OS with its own kernel, which provides stronger isolation but typically has slower startup times and higher overhead. Docker popularized containerization by simplifying the process of creating, sharing, and running containers.
