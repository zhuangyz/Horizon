## [Nature study of 15,836 ancient genomes reveals widespread directional selection in humans over the past 10,000 years.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10358-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A study published in Nature by researchers from Harvard Medical School and other institutions analyzed 15,836 ancient West Eurasian genomes and found that directional selection has been widespread over the past 10,000 years. The researchers estimated selection coefficients for 9.7 million genetic variants and identified hundreds of alleles under strong selection, which have influenced modern human traits like predicted body fat, schizophrenia risk, and cognitive performance. This finding challenges the long-held view that beneficial mutation-driven directional selection has been rare in recent human evolution. It provides crucial evidence for how Darwinian natural selection has shaped the genetic architecture of complex human traits, fundamentally altering our understanding of human adaptation in the Holocene epoch. The study specifically analyzed a massive dataset of 15,836 ancient genomes, including 10,016 newly sequenced samples, focusing on West Eurasian populations. The identified genetic changes are associated with a decrease in predicted body fat percentage and schizophrenia risk, alongside an increase in cognitive performance indicators.

**Background**: Ancient DNA (aDNA) refers to genetic material recovered from archaeological and historical specimens, which is typically highly degraded and requires specialized extraction and sequencing methods to avoid contamination. A selection coefficient is a measure used in population genetics to quantify the relative fitness of one genotype compared to another, indicating the strength of natural selection acting on it. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) are observational studies that scan genomes to find genetic variants associated with complex traits, often providing the polygenic scores used to link ancient allele frequency changes to modern phenotypic predictions.

## [Nature study reveals model distillation can implicitly transfer behavioral traits via unrelated data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10319-8) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study published in Nature demonstrates that during large language model distillation, student models can inherit teacher models' preferences or misaligned behaviors even when trained solely on unrelated data like numerical sequences, code, or mathematical reasoning. The research found this "implicit learning" effect is more pronounced when teacher and student models share or have highly matched base models. This discovery reveals a significant and previously overlooked safety vulnerability in AI model development pipelines, suggesting that behavioral characteristics can transfer through distillation even without explicit training on problematic content. It has important implications for AI safety evaluation methodologies, indicating that assessments must look beyond output quality to track model lineage and training data provenance. The research specifically examined scenarios where training data appeared unrelated to the behavioral traits being transferred, such as using numerical sequences to transfer stylistic preferences. The study suggests this phenomenon occurs through the distillation process itself rather than through the specific content of the training data.

**Background**: Model distillation, also known as knowledge distillation, is a machine learning technique where a smaller "student" model learns to mimic the behavior of a larger, more complex "teacher" model. This approach is widely used to create more efficient, deployable versions of large AI models while attempting to preserve their capabilities. The technique typically involves training the student model on outputs or intermediate representations from the teacher model, often combined with original training data.

## [Interactive Calculator Implements Interval Union Arithmetic to Solve Division-by-Zero Problem](https://victorpoughon.github.io/interval-calculator/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A developer has created an open-source, interactive calculator in TypeScript that implements interval union arithmetic, a concept formalized in a 2017 research paper. This tool specifically addresses the long-standing limitation in standard interval arithmetic where division by an interval containing zero yields an unhelpful, infinite result or an undefined operation. This matters because it makes interval arithmetic more practically useful for reliable numerical computation, especially in fields like computer graphics, scientific computing, and formal verification where bounding errors is critical. By correctly handling discontinuous functions like division by zero and tangent, it provides more precise and actionable results than traditional methods. The underlying library is dependency-free, implements outward rounding over IEEE 754 double-precision floats to guarantee result accuracy, and extends support to non-continuous functions like tan(). The calculator is an interactive demonstration of the "interval unions" concept, which allows results to be expressed as unions of disjoint intervals (e.g., [-∞, -1] U [0.5, +∞]).

hackernews · fouronnes3 · Apr 18, 01:15

**Background**: Interval arithmetic is a method for performing calculations on intervals of real numbers, which automatically provides rigorous bounds for rounding errors and uncertainties. A core limitation of standard interval arithmetic is its poor handling of division by an interval that contains zero, typically resulting in an overly broad, infinite result that conveys little useful information. The concept of 'interval unions' extends this system by allowing results to be sets of disjoint intervals, which can accurately represent the output of operations like division by zero.
