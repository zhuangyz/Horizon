## [Study provides first direct evidence of metal pollution from SpaceX rocket re-entry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-025-03154-8) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study published in Nature Communications Earth & Environment provides the first direct observational evidence that the uncontrolled re-entry of a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket stage created a significant plume of metal pollution in the upper atmosphere. Using lidar, scientists detected a tenfold spike in lithium atoms at 96 km altitude over Germany, which they traced back to the specific Falcon 9 debris using wind field modeling. This matters because it directly links the rapidly growing commercial space industry to a previously unquantified form of atmospheric pollution at critical altitudes. The findings raise concerns about the cumulative impact of thousands of planned satellite re-entries (like those from SpaceX's Starlink constellation) on the ozone layer and Earth's climate and radiation balance. The detected lithium plume contained approximately 30 kg of lithium, which is more than the annual amount delivered to Earth by natural meteoroids. The pollution lingered for over 20 minutes, and the source was identified as the aluminum-lithium alloy fuel tanks used in the Falcon 9's first stage.

telegram · zaihuapd · Feb 27, 17:15

**Background**: The Falcon 9 is a partially reusable rocket developed by SpaceX, with its first stage powered by nine Merlin engines and using aluminum-lithium alloy tanks. Lidar (Light Detection and Ranging) is a remote sensing technology that uses laser pulses to measure atmospheric constituents. When rocket stages or satellites re-enter the atmosphere uncontrollably, they burn up, potentially releasing metals and other chemicals into the mesosphere and lower thermosphere, regions that are difficult to monitor but crucial for atmospheric chemistry.
