## [FCC Expands Router Ban to Mobile Hotspots and CPE Devices](https://www.androidauthority.com/router-ban-expands-to-hotspots-3660505/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The FCC has updated its ban on foreign-made Wi-Fi routers to now include portable mobile hotspots (MiFi) and residential LTE/5G CPE devices. The ban applies only to new device applications and does not affect already-approved models, with exemptions for some vendors like Netgear until October 1, 2027. This expansion significantly broadens the regulatory impact on the consumer networking hardware market, potentially disrupting supply chains, reducing device availability, and raising prices for consumers. It also signals an intensifying geopolitical focus on technology supply chain security, particularly targeting Chinese-manufactured networking equipment. The ban currently exempts smartphones with hotspot functionality and enterprise-grade devices. The FCC has granted conditional exemptions to certain vendors, such as Netgear, allowing sales of affected products until October 1, 2027.

telegram · zaihuapd · Apr 25, 09:32

**Background**: CPE, or Customer Premises Equipment, refers to network terminal devices located at the user's end that connect to a telecom operator's services, such as routers, modems, and wireless gateways. In March 2026, the FCC first added foreign-made consumer routers to its restricted list, affecting an estimated 60% of routers sold in the U.S., most of which are manufactured in China. This new expansion extends those restrictions to portable hotspots and residential CPE devices, further tightening controls on foreign-made networking hardware.

## [China Regulates Fintech Marketing, Huabei and Baitiao Displays to Change](https://finance.sina.cn/stock/jdts/2026-04-25/detail-inhvsuve3987870.d.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

China's eight government departments jointly issued the 'Measures for the Management of Online Marketing of Financial Products,' which will take effect on September 30, 2026. The regulation requires non-bank payment institutions to separate loan products from payment tools at checkout, prioritizing the display of payment tools, and bans marketing phrases like 'low threshold' and 'instant approval.' This regulation directly impacts major credit payment products such as Huabei (Ant Group), Baitiao (JD.com), and Yuefu (Meituan), which are widely used by hundreds of millions of consumers in China. It aims to curb the bundling of payment and lending services, protect consumer rights, and promote fair competition in the fintech ecosystem. The regulation explicitly prohibits non-bank payment institutions from listing loan products as payment options at checkout. Marketing language such as 'low threshold,' 'instant credit,' and 'low interest rate' is also banned to prevent misleading promotions.

Products like Huabei and Baitiao are 'credit payment' services that allow consumers to buy now and pay later, often displayed prominently at checkout, blurring the line between payment and lending. The new regulation is part of a broader effort by Chinese authorities to tighten oversight of the fintech sector and reduce financial risks.
